WILLIAM D. STEGMEIER: PRESERVED SOURCES
Saved October 15, 2026 for The Life and Research of Agronomist William D. Stegmeier
https://nwsteg.github.io/2026/10/15/william-d-stegmeier.html

The research folder preserves the original family research collection, including the memorial, research papers, institutional histories, photographs, bibliography and missing-document list. Files marked EXCERPT are convenience extracts; a PARTIAL file is not a complete article. The record folder adds the 1940 childhood census, 1950 family research leads, North Dakota veterans register, thirteen matching Marine personnel pages and one adjacent unit heading. A 1954 reserve listing is not evidence of residence in Chicago. The 1950 lead does not establish Bill's own residence that year.

The botany folder adds government plant guides and a Kansas State guide supporting the original crop illustrations. The Canadian government biology document is a self-contained text snapshot with simplified formatting; the other guides are unchanged source PDFs. The previews folder contains cropped document reproductions used in the timeline and preserved as supporting evidence. Its provenance file records the source, PDF page and crop coordinates; the original documents are unchanged. The places folder contains the nine historical map views, their preserved source representations, web copies, photograph or publication dates, source URLs, copyright or license details, and a browsable credits page. Earlier photographs remain preserved with their own provenance and credits. The 1936 Grant County photograph is regional childhood context; the 1952 Elgin street scene is also available. Colorado State enrollment dates remain unconfirmed. The Garden City and Hays views are dated by their 1957 and 1984 publications; their exposure dates are unrecorded. The Hays web view extracts the upper photograph of a printed composite; the crop coordinates are recorded. Bill’s life dates and photograph dates are separate. The figures folder preserves the original editable SVG drawings and life map. The map-data folder contains the original Census boundary ZIP used to draw the map.

Sources copied from the original archive and the later downloaded record images are preserved byte for byte. Source attribution and existing notices are retained. Copyrights remain with the respective authors and publishers; this collection does not grant additional reuse rights. Source URLs, context, file sizes and SHA-256 checksums are in source-inventory.csv and source-inventory.json. SHA256SUMS.txt covers the individual files and inventories. The combined ZIP remains the preserved-file snapshot from earlier on October 16, 2026, before the research citation update below. Its embedded bibliography, inventories and checksums describe that snapshot. The separate bibliography, recovery-status files, inventories and checksums served beside the ZIP reflect the latest citation update; use those for the current list. No account credentials, private correspondence, or draft record requests are included.

To refresh the inventories and combined ZIP after an intentional source addition, run scripts/build_stegmeier_source_archive.py from the website repository. This does not download anything or modify the source documents.

HAYS STATION FEATURE
The 1984 Fort Hays Branch Station brochure is preserved in the biography folder. Its page-3 station aerial and office photographs illustrate Bill’s Hays years (1971–1998); publication date is established, but their exposure dates are unrecorded. Cropped JPEG views retain their original composite PNG, crop metadata, and full PDF. Bill’s field-day photograph retains its separate AgReport publication credit and unknown date.

BREEDING DIAGRAMS
Added October 16, 2026: figures/breeding-cycle.svg and figures/millet-family-tree.svg are editable vector explainers for the post. The cycle is a simplified illustration; the family tree follows the documented Hays stocks, ICRISAT selections, and HHB 67/68 releases in the archived 1998 parental-line papers. The family tree distinguishes the maintainer B lines from the separate pollen parents used to produce hybrids.
